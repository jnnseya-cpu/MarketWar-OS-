**GETTING FOUND ON CHATGPT: THE PAID REPORT COULD NOT SEE THE CRAWLER THAT DECIDES IT, AND THE PAGES WE HOST CARRIED NOTHING (§156).** Owner: *"we must have the mechanism to make both MarketWar itself and all businesses on the OS be able to be found on ChatGPT and AI search."* Most of it was already built — `ai-visibility.ts` asks the assistants, `geo-readiness.ts` audits a site, `ai-citation.ts` turns both into a playbook, `seo-artifacts.ts` generates per-brand JSON-LD and llms.txt. **Two things were wrong, and both were found by reading before writing.**

**ONE: TWO CRAWLER LISTS FOR THE SAME CONCEPT.** `shared/ai-readability.ts` knows ten crawlers by the token they honour in robots.txt and is used by the FREE audit. `backend/geo-readiness.ts` — the PAID GEO report, and the input `ai-citation.ts` builds its playbook from — carried its own list of six. The four it was missing included **`OAI-SearchBot`, the crawler that builds ChatGPT's search index**, plus `ChatGPT-User`, `Bingbot` (Copilot) and `Applebot-Extended`. **Demonstrated on real robots.txt bodies before anything was touched:** a site whose robots.txt says `User-agent: OAI-SearchBot / Disallow: /` was told by the paid report *"No AI crawler is blocked"* while the free audit on the same bytes said *"BLOCKED OAI-SearchBot"* — a customer paying for a GEO report cleared on the one question that decides whether they can appear in ChatGPT, and the playbook therefore never recommended the fix. Its parser was the weaker of the two as well: no `#` comment stripping and no last-group-wins, so a file that blocks everything then re-allows one crawler was read wrongly. Both deleted; `geo-readiness` now imports the one list and the one parser. **The evidence line also says HOW MANY were checked and what each feeds** — "No AI crawler is blocked" without a count is exactly how six masqueraded as all of them. Rule 6.

**TWO: WE GENERATED THE MACHINE-READABLE SURFACES AND NEVER SERVED THEM.** `seo-artifacts.ts` has always built per-brand Organization/LocalBusiness JSON-LD and an llms.txt — **as artifacts the customer copies onto a site hosted somewhere else.** The pages MarketWar *hosts* for them at `/b/{brandId}/{slug}` carried a title and a description and nothing more: no structured data, no canonical, no llms.txt, no sitemap. **So every page this platform serves on a customer's behalf was exactly as unreadable to an assistant as the sites the product marks them down for** — and our own report scores structured data at weight 25, FAQ content at 20 and llms.txt at 15. Now served, by reusing the generators rather than writing new ones: a single `@graph` per page (LocalBusiness/Organization + Product + WebPage + **FAQPage**, with `WebPage.publisher` pointing at the business so a recommendation can be attributed to anybody), a canonical, `/b/{brandId}/llms.txt` listing the brand's live pages, and `/b/{brandId}/sitemap.xml` with real `publishedAt` dates. **The per-brand sitemap respects the decision `app/robots.ts` already made** — customer pages are indexable but not ours to put in *our* sitemap — so discovery becomes possible without MarketWar claiming their content. **Drafts appear in none of it.** **The FAQ split is now ONE function** (`shared/faq-text.ts`) used by the accordion and the schema: markup that describes questions the page does not show is a Google policy violation and this codebase's oldest defect class, so there is no second implementation to drift from. **Three things fixed on the way:** the brand's `WebSite` node was being emitted with the brand's own domain on a page served from ours (claiming this page is part of a site it is not — dropped, the domain already declares one); I added a partial Open Graph block and **an existing guard caught it** (Next replaces `openGraph` wholesale, so a literal block loses the card image — routed through `openGraphFor`); and my own test asserted on a comment it had just stripped. **10 mutants killed. **THE BULK SENDER WAS SENDING MAIL THAT FAILS GMAIL'S AND YAHOO'S RULES (§159).** Owner: make the bulk sender — with and without attachments — work for us and for signed-up customers, and land in the main mailbox. **Attachments were already built** (10 per message, 20MB encoded cap, executables refused outright, one set per batch) and the MIME is correct: `multipart/mixed` wrapping `multipart/alternative`, the PDF byte-for-byte intact. **What was wrong was the header that decides placement.** Driven over a real TLS SMTP server, bulk messages came out carrying `List-ID` and `Feedback-ID` and **no `List-Unsubscribe` at all.** Since February 2024 that fails Gmail's and Yahoo's bulk-sender requirements outright, and an opt-out is separately required by PECR and CAN-SPAM. **`message-shape.ts` had NAMED it for months** — it pushes "List-Unsubscribe — required on bulk mail by Google, Yahoo, Apple and Microsoft" onto `missing` — and the message went out anyway, because nobody reads `missing` and the header was set only when a CALLER remembered to pass a URL. `/api/email` and `/api/newsletter` remembered; **`/api/review-requests` did not**, and nor did `placement-probe.ts` — which therefore measured a message shaped differently from the campaigns it exists to predict, the §140 defect in that very file wearing new clothes. **Fixed at the root: the mailer derives the sealed link itself**, because the mailer is the one place that knows a message is bulk (same lesson as §135's `transactional`), and **refuses to send bulk with no derivable opt-out** — a new `failure: "shape"`, distinct from `hygiene` so nobody is sent to clean a list that is fine. Refusing is the cheaper failure: **the sending pool's reputation is shared with every other tenant on it**, so one malformed campaign is charged against everybody else's inbox. 5 mutants killed, and one SURVIVED first — my test asserted only the true branch of the ternary, so a mutation replacing the else with `undefined` (exactly the old behaviour) left it green; half a ternary is half a check. **Driven: 31 proven / 0 broken (`npm run drive:bulk`)** — bulk with and without an attachment, one-to-one mail correctly carrying NO list headers, two tenants each getting their own `List-ID` and `Feedback-ID`, and the unsubscribe token per RECIPIENT so one link cannot opt out somebody else. **WHAT STILL CANNOT BE PROMISED: the Primary tab.** Nobody can — not Brevo, not anyone — because that is Gmail's classifier reading engagement history as well as shape. **THE RESEARCH FINDING THAT DECIDES THE WHATSAPP HALF: there is no longer any API that answers "is this number on WhatsApp".** The On-Premises `/contacts` endpoint answered it until client 2.45.1, when it began returning *valid* with a WhatsApp ID for EVERY number regardless of registration — it stopped being a check before it was switched off — and the whole On-Premises API was **sunset on 23 October 2025**. Everything that claims to bulk-check it drives WhatsApp Web through an unofficial wrapper, which breaches WhatsApp's terms and bans **the sending account** — here, the customer's own business number. **So it is not offered at any price**, for the same reason bought followers are not. And opt-in is not optional: WhatsApp's Business Messaging Policy requires permission before any business-initiated message, so a found number is not a send list. `shared/whatsapp-reach.ts` therefore answers the narrower question it can answer honestly — *is this a dialable mobile, and what is the compliant way to open a conversation* — carries `needsOptIn: true` on **every** verdict so no surface can forget it, and the one-click action for a number with no opt-in is a `wa.me` link **the prospect follows to message you first**. A landline and a VoIP number get no link at all. `toE164` **refuses a national number with no country** rather than writing `44` on the front of it, because a guessed prefix dials a real stranger.

## 7. Rules that outrank preference

Full standard: `docs/ENGINEERING-DIRECTIVE.md`. `CLAUDE.md` loads every session and carries the additive-only law, the margin floor and the no-fabrication rule. Only here:

- **Verify before shipping:** typecheck, build, layers, lint, tests — then MUTATE the new
  tests, AND READ THE CI RUN. A test that has never failed is not evidence.
- **`npm run verify` now runs `check:advisories`** — high or critical in a SHIPPED dependency fails the
  gate. It did NOT before, which is how two criticals sat under a green gate. **An unreachable
  registry prints UNKNOWN and does not pass.** **A security step must never sit behind a step that can
  fail on its own** — `if: always()`.
- **"IMPENETRABLE" IS NOT A CLAIM ANYONE CAN MAKE, AND WILL NOT BE MADE HERE.** What is true is
  narrower and checkable: deny-by-default rules, server-side tenant scoping, field encryption at rest,
  no secret in repo/bundle/log/URL, a gate that fails on a shipped advisory. **End-to-end encryption
  does not apply where the server must read the data**; `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_MASTER_KEY` is the control.
- **`requireAuth` returning `ok` does NOT mean the scope was checked** — with no Firebase Admin it
  returns `{ok:true, enforced:false}` first. Any surface touching real customer data needs `enforced`.
- **Drive the routes, do not read them** — a production build on `next start` found an open endpoint
  and a 404 on the paid-conversion path that no reading had.
- **Anything scoped to a tenant is scoped by the RECORD, never by a constant** (§6), followed through
  every reader. **Point our own audit at our own pages** before claiming the marketing surface is fine.
- **The owner does not use, and will not be offered, a third-party ESP** — MarketWar's own sending
  pool is the product. And **never invent a company detail** (number, address, phone, handle, trading
  name): a blank is recoverable, a fabricated one on a page that sells is not.
- **Never tell the owner to do by hand what the platform should do for them.** When the answer is "go
  and look", the defect is that nothing is looking; when it is "go and click", nothing is clicking.
  **Before calling anything unfinishable, ask the narrower question** — the commercial loop was written
  off as needing a human when it only needed money, and only for half of it (§143).
- Push to `claude/marketwar-os-platform-xrgg5r` and mirror to `main` file-by-file, never
  by merge. The branches no longer differ on anything — a diff between them is a mistake, not a plan.
